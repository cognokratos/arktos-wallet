## 2. What Went Well? (Successes & Strengths)

-   **Strong Foundational Implementation**: Successful delivery of core wallet creation and multi-account management (Stories 1.1 & 1.2), providing a robust base for future epics.
-   **Effective Key Derivation**: The BIP32 hierarchical derivation for accounts was successfully implemented and validated, ensuring a standard and scalable approach.
-   **Proactive Security Improvement**: A potential security concern regarding custom encryption was identified and proactively resolved during Epic 1 (switching from custom Keccak-256 XOR to `Aes256Gcm` in Story 1.3), demonstrating effective risk mitigation.
-   **Robust API Key Authentication**: Secure and efficient API key authentication and management were established (Story 1.3), critical for agent-based interaction.
-   **Production-Ready Dockerization**: Efficient and secure Dockerization using `cargo-chef` and `distroless` (Story 1.4) was a key enabler for deployment readiness.
-   **Comprehensive Documentation**: The project documentation was significantly enhanced (Story 1.5), providing valuable guides for customization and regional compliance, solidifying its "educational blueprint" purpose.
-   **High-Quality Development**: Consistent and comprehensive test coverage (unit, integration, and even Docker build tests) across all functionalities was maintained, reflecting a high quality bar.
-   **Architectural Adherence**: Strong adherence to defined architectural patterns and decisions across all stories.

---

## 3. What Didn't Go Well? (Challenges & Growth Areas)

-   **Initial Custom Encryption Choice**: An initial choice for a non-standard custom encryption method for passphrases (Story 1.1) was identified as a potential risk. **(Note: This was successfully addressed in Story 1.3, turning it into a learning experience rather than an ongoing challenge.)**
-   **Knowledge Sharing Gaps**: An instance of a team member being unaware of a significant security improvement (the encryption refactor in Story 1.3) highlighted a need for better internal communication channels.

---

## 4. Key Insights & Learnings

-   **Iterative Process Effectiveness**: The iterative development and review process allowed the team to identify and mitigate a critical security risk (custom cryptography) early in the epic, showcasing the value of continuous feedback loops.
-   **Importance of Foundational Work**: Investing in clear architectural definitions and consistent implementation patterns early on paid dividends in predictability and quality throughout the epic.
-   **Anticipating Future Needs**: The existing `derive_account_keys` function, which already supports Bitcoin and Ethereum, significantly streamlines the upcoming Epic 2, demonstrating good foresight.

## 5. Next Epic Preview (Epic 2: Address Generation and Retrieval)

**Epic 2 Title:** Address Generation and Retrieval

**Key Discovery & Impact:**
It was clarified during this retrospective that the necessary key derivation logic for Bitcoin and Ethereum public addresses is *already implemented* in `src/wallet_manager.rs` (from Epic 1). This significantly reduces the technical complexity of Epic 2.

**Impact on Epic 2 Planning:**
-   Fewer complex derivation tasks will be required.
-   Implementation will primarily focus on building the MCP tools and performing the final blockchain-specific address formatting from the existing public keys.
-   Story estimates for Epic 2 may be adjusted to reflect this reduced scope for derivation work.
